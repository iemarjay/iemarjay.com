## Overview

Short-form video content to build personal brand, showcase expertise, and attract opportunities. Target: look successful, fun, and intelligent while sharing developer and security insights.

**Platforms**: X (video), TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels
**Format**: 30-90 second vertical videos
**Frequency**: 2-3 videos/week (start with 1/week to build consistency)

---

## Visual Brand

### Clothing Guidelines

**Go-to looks:**
- Solid color t-shirts (navy, black, forest green, burgundy)
- Well-fitted button-up shirts with rolled sleeves
- Simple crew neck sweaters for texture
- Clean, minimal accessories (watch, simple chain)

**Avoid:**
- Thin stripes (causes moire effect on camera)
- All white (blows out on camera)
- Logos/text (distracting + potential copyright issues)
- Busy patterns

**Tip**: Pick 3-4 "filming shirts" and rotate for consistency/recognition.

### Background Setup

**Primary setup options:**

1. **Clean wall + plant**
   - Plain wall (white, cream, or muted color)
   - One plant for life/color
   - Minimal, professional look

2. **Desk setup shot**
   - Monitor, keyboard visible
   - Developer aesthetic
   - Shows workspace authenticity

3. **Bookshelf corner** (optional)
   - Shows depth, looks intellectual
   - Don't overdo the props

**Lighting (critical):**
- Face a window for natural light (golden hour ideal)
- Ring light positioned in front (budget: N5-15k)
- Avoid overhead lighting only (creates under-eye shadows)

**Quick wins:**
- Declutter the frame completely
- One colored LED strip behind for depth (optional)
- Consistent background = brand recognition

## Content Series

### 1. "Bug of the Week"
**Format**: 30-60 seconds
**Structure**:
- Hook: "This bug cost [amount/impact]..."
- Show: Simplified code/diagram
- Explain: What went wrong
- Lesson: How to prevent it

### 2. "Code Review Red Flags"
**Format**: 15-30 seconds
**Structure**:
- Show code snippet
- Point out the red flag
- Quick explanation why

### 3. "Security Storytime"
**Format**: 60-90 seconds
**Structure**:
- Dramatic hook
- Build tension with the problem
- Resolution/lesson

### 4. "Building Shuttershow"
**Format**: 30-60 seconds
**Structure**:
- What I built this week
- Challenge I faced
- How I solved it (or asking for input)

### 5. "Dev Life Lagos"
**Format**: 60 seconds
**Structure**:
- Day in the life snippets
- Workspace/setup content
- Local tech scene highlights

## Priority Videos

### Video #1: Introduction

**Hook options:**
- "I cost a client $10,000... and it made me a better developer"
- "8 years of code, one expensive lesson"
- "Lagos developer here — let me tell you how I turned my biggest failure into my superpower"

**Script (60 seconds):**

```
[0-5s] HOOK
"I once wrote a bug that cost a client $10,000."

[5-15s] WHO I AM
"I'm Emmanuel — full-stack developer, 8 years in, based in Lagos, Nigeria."

[15-25s] WHAT I DO
"I build web2 and web3 systems. Fintech backends, smart contracts,
the stuff where bugs actually cost money."

[25-40s] THE TWIST
"That $10k failure? It broke me... in a good way.
I became obsessed with security. Built audit methodologies.
Started finding vulnerabilities before they find you."

[40-55s] THE PROOF
"160+ issues found. Millions protected.
From causing the problem to preventing them."

[55-60s] CTA
"Follow for dev content, security insights, and building in public."
```

### Video #2: The $10k Bug Story

**Hook**: "The most expensive bug I ever wrote"

**Script (90 seconds):**

```
[0-5s] HOOK
"A webhook bug cost my client $10,000. Here's what I learned."

[5-20s] SETUP
"I built a payment system. Thought it was solid.
Then someone discovered you could replay webhook requests...
and they did. $10,000 gone."

[20-35s] IMPACT
"My client lost money. I lost their trust.
And I couldn't sleep for weeks.
That moment broke something in me — in a good way."

[35-55s] TRANSFORMATION
"I became obsessed with security.
Studied every vulnerability class.
Built audit methodologies.
Learned to think like an attacker."

[55-75s] REDEMPTION
"Months later, they called me back. This time to audit their systems.
I found 24 vulnerabilities — including the exact type that hit us before.
Did forensic analysis. Tracked $7,000 in fraud. Found $5,000 still recoverable."

[75-90s] LESSON
"The lesson? Your worst failure can become your greatest expertise.
Now I find the bugs before the hackers do."
```

### Video #3: What Replay Attacks Actually Look Like

**Hook**: "This is how I lost a client $10k in 30 seconds"

**Script (45 seconds):**

```
[0-5s] HOOK
"This is how a replay attack works — and why it cost $10,000."

[5-20s] EXPLAIN
"Payment webhook comes in. 'User paid $100.'
Server credits the account. Done.
But what if you... capture that webhook... and send it again?
Server sees 'User paid $100.' Credits again.
And again. And again."

[20-35s] WHY IT HAPPENED
"No signature verification. No idempotency key.
The server just trusted every request."

[35-45s] THE FIX
"Now? Unique transaction IDs. Signature verification.
One request, one credit. Always."
```

## Filming Tips

- **Eye contact**: Look at the camera lens, not the screen
- **Energy**: 20% more than feels natural (camera flattens energy)
- **Pace**: Slightly faster than conversation (keeps attention)
- **Captions**: Always add them (80%+ watch without sound)
- **Hook**: First 3 seconds determine if people watch
- **Lighting**: Most important technical element

## Posting Strategy

### Cross-posting approach:

1. **Film once** in vertical format
2. **Edit** with captions and any graphics
3. **Post natively** to each platform (not cross-links)
4. **Adapt captions** per platform style:
   - X: Shorter, conversational
   - TikTok: More casual, hashtags
   - YouTube Shorts: SEO-focused title
   - Instagram: Polished, hashtags in comments

### Optimal posting times (Nigerian timezone):
- X: 8-9 AM, 12-1 PM, 6-8 PM WAT
- TikTok: 7-9 PM WAT
- YouTube: 2-4 PM WAT
- Instagram: 11 AM-1 PM, 7-9 PM WAT
